## 📊 What you're actually being scored on

Each elective is marked **independently** out of 500:

```
        ┌──────────────────────────────────────────────┐
        │  General English & GK        100 marks       │  ← shared across all three
        ├──────────────────────────────────────────────┤
        │  MCQ (100 Q × 2)             200 marks       │  ← 40% of the total!
        │  Descriptive Paper-I         100 marks       │
        │  Descriptive Paper-II        100 marks       │
        ├──────────────────────────────────────────────┤
        │  TOTAL per elective          500 marks       │
        └──────────────────────────────────────────────┘
```

Across three electives you sit **10 papers totalling 1,600 marks of examination**.

### Three facts that should drive everything you do

1. **The MCQ is worth 200 — double any descriptive paper.** It is the single biggest
   scoring block and the easiest to prepare for. Most candidates over-invest in
   descriptive writing and under-invest here. Don't.

2. **Negative marking is 1/3 of 2 marks = −0.667 per wrong answer.** A blind guess on
   a 4-option question has expected value `(0.25 × 2) − (0.75 × 0.667) = 0` — exactly
   break-even. So: **guess only when you can eliminate at least one option.** One
   eliminated option makes guessing clearly positive. Pure blind guessing is a waste.

3. **The descriptive papers give you choice.** Section A offers 10 questions and you
   answer 8. Section B offers 5, you answer 3. Section C offers 4, you answer 2.
   You can drop your two weakest topics in every single section and still score 100%.
   **This means you do not need perfect syllabus coverage — you need solid coverage of
   about 70% of it.** Plan accordingly.

## 🎒 Exam-day logistics checklist

- [ ] Download and print the **admit card** as soon as NPSC releases it
- [ ] Confirm your **General paper venue** against your roll number (table above)
- [ ] Confirm **NPSC Exam Hall, Kohima** location and travel time for the 7 technical papers
- [ ] Note the **two double-header days**: 30 Sep and 12 Oct (descriptive 9–12, then MCQ 1–3) and 13 Oct (descriptive 9–12, then MCQ 1–3). Pack food and water — these are long days.
- [ ] Black/blue ball pens (several), HB pencils + eraser for OMR, permitted ID
- [ ] Check NPSC's instructions on whether a non-programmable calculator is permitted
- [ ] Re-check the routine and any corrigendum on npsc.nagaland.gov.in the week before —
      **NPSC has already issued three corrigenda to this advertisement** (10, 15 and 17 July 2026),
      so dates can move.

# GK MCQ Bank — Practice Questions for Paper 1 (General English & GK)

> **Exam:** NPSC CTSE 2026, General English & GK (Combined) — 100 marks, 3 hours,
> **26 September 2026.** GK is 50 of the 100 marks. This file is a **self-test bank**:
> 160 original multiple-choice questions across every GK topic in the official syllabus,
> plus a current-affairs "what to look up" block. Pair it with `03_GK_CAPSULE.md` for the
> underlying facts and explanations — this file is for drilling recall speed.

## How this paper actually tests GK — and how to use this file

- The syllabus line is: *"General knowledge comprising of current events, general
  science, mental ability, Indian history, Indian polity, Indian economy, Geography with
  combination of objective type and short descriptive answers — 50 Marks."* In practice
  (see the real 2025 paper) that means a long run of **single-best-answer, four-option
  MCQs** — some straightforward recall, some "which of these statements is true" /
  "which of i, ii, iii is correct" combination items — answered **in a grid on the answer
  booklet**, not written out.
- **⚠️ No negative marking applies to the General paper — verify this on your actual
  2026 question paper's instruction page before you rely on it.** The real 2025 General
  paper's cover page states *"There will be no negative marking"* in black and white, and
  this is a **different rule from the Technical MCQ paper**, which does negative-mark.
  Because the rule can only be confirmed from the paper you are actually sitting, treat
  "no negative marking" as *likely but not certified* until you read your own booklet's
  instructions on exam day — and if it does turn out to carry negative marking, do not
  blind-guess; only answer once you can eliminate at least one option.
- **Self-test discipline:** cover the answer key, attempt each section's block cold,
  then open the `<details>` key. Re-attempt only what you got wrong, a few days later.
- **Your structural edge:** you are a Naga sitting a Nagaland exam. The Nagaland & North-
  East section below is deliberately the largest block — a national candidate cannot
  match you here, so over-learn it.
- **Accuracy discipline in this file:** every fact that can change over time (current
  office-holders, district counts, scheme outlays, population figures) is marked
  **⚠️ verify** rather than stated as fixed truth — re-check any such figure from a
  current source before writing it in the real exam. Nothing in this file is invented;
  where an exact current fact was needed and could not be verified, a question **stem**
  is given instead so you know what to look up.

<summary><b>Answer key — Section 1 (Polity)</b></summary>

1. **C** — Adopted 26 Nov 1949; it *came into force* on 26 Jan 1950 (a classic trap: the two dates are often swapped in questions).
2. **B** — "Socialist, Secular, Integrity" were added; the original Preamble already had Sovereign/Democratic/Republic and Justice/Liberty/Equality/Fraternity.
3. **C** — Article 32 (Right to Constitutional Remedies) lets a citizen move the Supreme Court directly to enforce Fundamental Rights.
4. **B** — Right to Property was removed from Part III and demoted to a legal right under Art. 300A.
5. **C** — Quo Warranto = "by what authority" — challenges an unauthorised holding of public office.
6. **B** — DPSPs (Art. 37) are explicitly non-justiciable, unlike Fundamental Rights.
7. **B** — The Swaran Singh Committee recommended Fundamental Duties, added by the 42nd Amendment, inspired by the USSR constitution.
8. **C** — Money Bills (Art. 110) can be introduced only in the Lok Sabha; the Rajya Sabha can only recommend changes within 14 days.
9. **B** — The CAG (Art. 148) audits Union and State government accounts.
10. **C** — *Kesavananda Bharati v. State of Kerala* (1973) established the Basic Structure Doctrine.
11. **B** — The ground was changed from "internal disturbance" to "armed rebellion" by the 44th Amendment, to prevent misuse of Emergency powers (as happened in 1975).
12. **B** — Articles 20 (protection re conviction) and 21 (life & personal liberty) remain in force even in a National Emergency.
13. **C** — NITI Aayog is neither a constitutional body (not in the Constitution) nor a statutory body (no Act created it) — it is an executive resolution body, like the Planning Commission it replaced.
14. **C** — Gram Panchayat (village) → Panchayat Samiti (block) → Zilla Parishad (district) is the three-tier order; Gram Panchayat is the lowest.
15. **B** — Article 371A is Nagaland's own special-provisions article.
16. **B** — Inserted by the 13th Constitutional Amendment Act, 1962.
17. **B** — 1 December 1963, the day Nagaland became India's 16th state.
18. **B** — The four protected subjects are: religious/social practice, Naga customary law, administration of justice per Naga customary law, and ownership/transfer of land and its resources.
19. **B** — The Governor's individual-judgement special responsibility under 371A concerns law and order during internal disturbances.
20. **C** — Article 243M excludes Nagaland (along with Meghalaya and Mizoram) from Part IX; Assam, Tripura and Sikkim are not among the states excluded.
21. **D** — Article 371G is Mizoram's special-provisions article (do not confuse with Nagaland's 371A).
22. **B** — Nagaland runs local governance through traditional Village Councils and VDBs, plus the Communitisation Act, 2002, rather than the standard Panchayati Raj structure.
23. **A** — The Finance Commission is constituted under Article 280, every five years.
24. **B** — The Rajya Sabha is a permanent body; one-third retire every two years. It cannot be dissolved, cannot originate Money Bills, and its presiding officer is the Vice-President (Chairman), not the Speaker.
25. **C** — The 101st Amendment (2016) introduced GST, effective 1 July 2017.

## §1 — INDIAN POLITY

**1.** Untouchability is abolished under:  
(a) Art. 14 (b) Art. 15 (c) Art. 17 (d) Art. 18
> **Ans: (c) Article 17.**

**2.** Fundamental Rights are contained in:  
(a) Part II (b) Part III (c) Part IV (d) Part IVA
> **Ans: (b) Part III** (Arts. 12–35). Part IV = DPSP; Part IVA = Fundamental Duties.

**3.** Under Art. 79, Parliament of India consists of:  
(a) Lok Sabha and Rajya Sabha (b) President, Lok Sabha and Rajya Sabha (c) PM and both Houses (d) Lok Sabha only
> **Ans: (b)** The President is a part of Parliament.

**4.** A joint sitting of both Houses (Art. 108) is presided over by:  
(a) President (b) Vice-President (c) Speaker of Lok Sabha (d) Prime Minister
> **Ans: (c) Speaker of the Lok Sabha.**

**5.** The President's power to grant pardon is under:  
(a) Art. 72 (b) Art. 123 (c) Art. 143 (d) Art. 161
> **Ans: (a) Article 72.** (A Governor's is Art. 161; ordinances are Art. 123.)

**6.** The Election Commission of India is provided for under:  
(a) Art. 280 (b) Art. 315 (c) Art. 324 (d) Art. 356
> **Ans: (c) Article 324.** (UPSC = Art. 315.)

**7.** The oath of office to the President of India is administered by:  
(a) Vice-President (b) Chief Justice of India (c) Speaker (d) Prime Minister
> **Ans: (b) Chief Justice of India.**

**8.** The Council of Ministers is collectively responsible to:  
(a) President (b) Rajya Sabha (c) Lok Sabha (d) Parliament as a whole
> **Ans: (c) Lok Sabha** (Art. 75(3)).

**9.** The maximum strength of the Rajya Sabha is:  
(a) 238 (b) 245 (c) 250 (d) 552
> **Ans: (c) 250** (238 elected + 12 nominated by the President).

**10.** How many languages are in the Eighth Schedule?  
(a) 14 (b) 18 (c) 22 (d) 24
> **Ans: (c) 22.**

**11.** The idea of a Concurrent List was borrowed from:  
(a) USA (b) Canada (c) Australia (d) Ireland
> **Ans: (c) Australia.** (Single citizenship → UK; Fundamental Rights → USA; Emergency → Germany/Weimar; Fundamental Duties → USSR.)

**12.** The writ "Habeas Corpus" literally means:  
(a) We command (b) To have the body (c) By what authority (d) To be certified
> **Ans: (b) "To have the body".** (Mandamus = we command; Quo Warranto = by what authority; Certiorari = to be certified.)

**13.** The 74th Amendment (1992) deals with:  
(a) Panchayats (b) Municipalities (c) Cooperative societies (d) Anti-defection
> **Ans: (b) Municipalities** (12th Schedule). The 73rd = Panchayats (11th Schedule).

**14.** The Governor of a state is appointed by:  
(a) Chief Minister (b) Prime Minister (c) President (d) Chief Justice
> **Ans: (c) The President** (Art. 155).

**15.** The first Speaker of the Lok Sabha was:  
(a) M. A. Ayyangar (b) G. V. Mavalankar (c) Hukam Singh (d) N. Sanjiva Reddy
> **Ans: (b) G. V. Mavalankar.**

**16.** The first Chief Election Commissioner of India was:  
(a) T. N. Seshan (b) Sukumar Sen (c) K. V. K. Sundaram (d) S. P. Sen Verma
> **Ans: (b) Sukumar Sen.**

**17.** The 106th Amendment (2023, "Nari Shakti Vandan") provides:  
(a) 50% reservation in Panchayats (b) one-third reservation for women in Lok Sabha and State Assemblies (c) reservation for women in jobs (d) equal pay
> **Ans: (b).**

**18.** 10% reservation for Economically Weaker Sections (EWS) was introduced by the:  
(a) 101st (b) 102nd (c) 103rd (d) 104th Amendment
> **Ans: (c) 103rd Amendment, 2019.**

**19.** The first meeting of the Constituent Assembly was held on:  
(a) 26 Nov 1949 (b) 9 Dec 1946 (c) 15 Aug 1947 (d) 26 Jan 1946
> **Ans: (b) 9 December 1946.** The temporary president was Dr. Sachchidananda Sinha.

**20.** A Money Bill is defined under:  
(a) Art. 108 (b) Art. 110 (c) Art. 112 (d) Art. 117
> **Ans: (b) Article 110.** (Art. 112 = Annual Financial Statement / Budget.)

**21.** Who decides whether a bill is a Money Bill?  
(a) President (b) Finance Minister (c) Speaker of Lok Sabha (d) Chairman of Rajya Sabha
> **Ans: (c) The Speaker**, and the decision is final.

**22.** The Comptroller and Auditor General is appointed under:  
(a) Art. 76 (b) Art. 148 (c) Art. 165 (d) Art. 280
> **Ans: (b) Article 148.** (Attorney General = Art. 76; Advocate General = Art. 165.)

## §2 — NAGALAND & NORTH-EAST

**23.** The first general election to the Nagaland Legislative Assembly was held in:  
(a) 1963 (b) 1964 (c) 1969 (d) 1972
> **Ans: (b) January 1964.**

**24.** By population, the largest Naga tribe is generally cited as the:  
(a) Ao (b) Angami (c) Konyak (d) Sumi
> **Ans: (c) Konyak.**

**25.** Sükrünyi is the festival of the:  
(a) Chakhesang (b) Rengma (c) Pochury (d) Zeliang
> **Ans: (a) Chakhesang** (January).

**26.** Ngada is the festival of the:  
(a) Rengma (b) Phom (c) Chang (d) Lotha
> **Ans: (a) Rengma** (November).

**27.** Naknyulem is the festival of the:  
(a) Konyak (b) Chang (c) Sangtam (d) Yimkhiung
> **Ans: (b) Chang** (July).

**28.** Monyü is the festival of the:  
(a) Phom (b) Konyak (c) Khiamniungan (d) Ao
> **Ans: (a) Phom** (April).

**29.** Hega is a festival of the:  
(a) Zeliang (b) Kuki (c) Kachari (d) Garo
> **Ans: (a) Zeliang.**

**30.** Tsokum is the festival of the:  
(a) Sangtam (b) Khiamniungan (c) Yimkhiung (d) Pochury
> **Ans: (b) Khiamniungan** (October).

**31.** The name "Kisama" (Naga Heritage Village) comes from the two villages:  
(a) Kigwema and Phesama (b) Kiruphema and Sazolie (c) Kidima and Sechü (d) Khonoma and Jakhama
> **Ans: (a) Kigwema + Phesama.**

**32.** Among Nagaland's wildlife sanctuaries, Fakim is in:  
(a) Kohima (b) Kiphire (c) Mon (d) Dimapur
> **Ans: (b) Kiphire.** (Puliebadze = Kohima; Rangapahar = Dimapur.)

**33.** Nagaland lies mostly in which physiographic region?  
(a) Shillong Plateau (b) Patkai–Naga Hills (c) Mishmi Hills (d) Lushai Hills
> **Ans: (b) The Patkai range / Naga Hills.**

**34.** Kohima Village (Bara Basti) is often described as:  
(a) the smallest village in India (b) one of the largest villages in Asia (c) India's first smart village (d) Nagaland's only Angami village
> **Ans: (b).**

**35.** Manipur, Meghalaya and Tripura became full states on:  
(a) 1 Dec 1963 (b) 21 Jan 1972 (c) 20 Feb 1987 (d) 16 May 1975
> **Ans: (b) 21 January 1972.**

**36.** Mizoram and Arunachal Pradesh became full states in:  
(a) 1972 (b) 1975 (c) 1987 (d) 1963
> **Ans: (c) 1987** (20 February 1987).

**37.** Sikkim became a state of India in 1975 by the:  
(a) 35th (b) 36th (c) 42nd (d) 13th Amendment
> **Ans: (b) 36th Amendment.** It was the 22nd state.

**38.** Capital of Arunachal Pradesh:  
(a) Tawang (b) Itanagar (c) Pasighat (d) Ziro
> **Ans: (b) Itanagar.** Other NE capitals: Shillong, Aizawl, Agartala, Gangtok, Dispur, Imphal and Kohima.

**39.** The Siliguri Corridor ("Chicken's Neck") connects the North-East to the rest of India through:  
(a) Bihar (b) West Bengal (c) Sikkim (d) Assam
> **Ans: (b) West Bengal.**

**40.** Kaziranga National Park is famous for the:  
(a) Asiatic lion (b) one-horned rhinoceros (c) snow leopard (d) Sangai deer
> **Ans: (b) One-horned rhinoceros.**

**41.** The Dhola–Sadiya Bridge (Bhupen Hazarika Setu) is on the river:  
(a) Brahmaputra (b) Lohit (c) Barak (d) Subansiri
> **Ans: (b) Lohit** (Assam–Arunachal). Bogibeel is India's longest rail-cum-road bridge.

**42.** The Mizo Peace Accord was signed in:  
(a) 1975 (b) 1986 (c) 1987 (d) 1997
> **Ans: (b) 1986.**

**43.** Living root bridges are a famous feature of:  
(a) Nagaland (b) Meghalaya (c) Sikkim (d) Tripura
> **Ans: (b) Meghalaya.**

**44.** The Battle of Saraighat (1671), where the Ahoms defeated the Mughals, was led by:  
(a) Lachit Borphukan (b) Sukaphaa (c) Rudra Singha (d) Gadadhar Singha
> **Ans: (a) Lachit Borphukan.** (Sukaphaa founded the Ahom kingdom in 1228.)

**45.** Losar, the New Year festival of the Monpas, is celebrated in:  
(a) Nagaland (b) Arunachal Pradesh (c) Mizoram (d) Manipur
> **Ans: (b) Arunachal Pradesh** (and Sikkim/Ladakh). Chapchar Kut = Mizoram; Wangala = Garo, Meghalaya.

**46.** The Stilwell (Ledo) Road of WWII ran from Ledo in Assam to:  
(a) Mandalay (b) Kunming, China (c) Lhasa (d) Dhaka
> **Ans: (b) Kunming, China** (via Burma).

## §3 — INDIAN HISTORY

**47.** Harappa was first excavated in 1921 by:  
(a) R. D. Banerji (b) Daya Ram Sahni (c) John Marshall (d) Mortimer Wheeler
> **Ans: (b) Daya Ram Sahni.** (Mohenjo-daro, 1922 → R. D. Banerji.)

**48.** The Harappan dockyard was found at:  
(a) Kalibangan (b) Lothal (c) Dholavira (d) Ropar
> **Ans: (b) Lothal** (Gujarat).

**49.** The oldest Veda is the:  
(a) Samaveda (b) Yajurveda (c) Rigveda (d) Atharvaveda
> **Ans: (c) Rigveda.**

**50.** Buddha delivered his first sermon at:  
(a) Bodh Gaya (b) Sarnath (c) Kushinagar (d) Lumbini
> **Ans: (b) Sarnath.** (Born at Lumbini; enlightened at Bodh Gaya; died at Kushinagar.)

**51.** The 24th and last Tirthankara of Jainism was:  
(a) Rishabhanatha (b) Parshvanatha (c) Mahavira (d) Neminatha
> **Ans: (c) Mahavira.**

**52.** Ashoka embraced Buddhism after the war of:  
(a) Magadha (b) Kalinga (c) Hydaspes (d) Taxila
> **Ans: (b) Kalinga** (c. 261 BCE).

**53.** "Indica" was written by:  
(a) Fa-Hien (b) Megasthenes (c) Kautilya (d) Hiuen Tsang
> **Ans: (b) Megasthenes**, Seleucus's envoy to Chandragupta Maurya. (Arthashastra → Kautilya.)

**54.** The Chinese pilgrim who visited India during Harshavardhana's reign was:  
(a) Fa-Hien (b) Hiuen Tsang (c) I-Tsing (d) Al-Biruni
> **Ans: (b) Hiuen Tsang (Xuanzang).**

**55.** The construction of the Qutb Minar was begun by:  
(a) Iltutmish (b) Qutb-ud-din Aibak (c) Alauddin Khalji (d) Balban
> **Ans: (b) Qutb-ud-din Aibak.** It was completed by Iltutmish.

**56.** Din-i-Ilahi was started by:  
(a) Babur (b) Humayun (c) Akbar (d) Aurangzeb
> **Ans: (c) Akbar** (1582).

**57.** The Battle of Plassey (1757) was fought between the British and:  
(a) Mir Qasim (b) Siraj-ud-Daulah (c) Tipu Sultan (d) Shuja-ud-Daulah
> **Ans: (b) Siraj-ud-Daulah.** (Buxar, 1764 → Mir Qasim, Shuja-ud-Daulah and Shah Alam II.)

**58.** The Permanent Settlement of Bengal (1793) was introduced by:  
(a) Warren Hastings (b) Lord Cornwallis (c) Lord Wellesley (d) Lord Dalhousie
> **Ans: (b) Lord Cornwallis.**

**59.** The "Doctrine of Lapse" is associated with:  
(a) Lord Dalhousie (b) Lord Curzon (c) Lord Ripon (d) Lord Canning
> **Ans: (a) Lord Dalhousie.**

**60.** Sati was abolished in 1829 by:  
(a) Lord William Bentinck (b) Lord Dalhousie (c) Lord Minto (d) Lord Lytton
> **Ans: (a) Lord William Bentinck**, with the support of Raja Ram Mohan Roy.

**61.** The Revolt of 1857 began at:  
(a) Delhi (b) Kanpur (c) Meerut (d) Barrackpore
> **Ans: (c) Meerut** (10 May 1857). (Mangal Pandey's act at Barrackpore was in March 1857.)

**62.** The Partition of Bengal (1905) was carried out by:  
(a) Lord Minto (b) Lord Curzon (c) Lord Hardinge (d) Lord Ripon
> **Ans: (b) Lord Curzon.** It was annulled in 1911.

**63.** Gandhiji's first satyagraha in India was at:  
(a) Kheda (b) Ahmedabad (c) Champaran (d) Bardoli
> **Ans: (c) Champaran** (1917), for indigo farmers.

**64.** The Non-Cooperation Movement was withdrawn after the incident at:  
(a) Jallianwala Bagh (b) Chauri Chaura (c) Dandi (d) Bardoli
> **Ans: (b) Chauri Chaura** (February 1922).

**65.** The "Purna Swaraj" resolution was passed at the Lahore session (1929), presided over by:  
(a) Mahatma Gandhi (b) Jawaharlal Nehru (c) Motilal Nehru (d) Subhas Bose
> **Ans: (b) Jawaharlal Nehru.** 26 January 1930 was observed as Independence Day.

**66.** The Poona Pact (1932) was signed between Gandhiji and:  
(a) Jinnah (b) B. R. Ambedkar (c) Lord Irwin (d) Tej Bahadur Sapru
> **Ans: (b) Dr. B. R. Ambedkar.**

**67.** "Give me blood, and I will give you freedom" was said by:  
(a) Bhagat Singh (b) Subhas Chandra Bose (c) Bal Gangadhar Tilak (d) Lala Lajpat Rai
> **Ans: (b) Subhas Chandra Bose.** ("Swaraj is my birthright" → Tilak.)

**68.** The first Indian Governor-General of independent India was:  
(a) Lord Mountbatten (b) C. Rajagopalachari (c) Rajendra Prasad (d) Sardar Patel
> **Ans: (b) C. Rajagopalachari** (the last Governor-General as well).

## §4 — INDIAN ECONOMY

**69.** The Repo Rate is the rate at which:  
(a) banks lend to the public (b) RBI lends short-term funds to commercial banks (c) banks park surplus with RBI (d) government borrows abroad
> **Ans: (b).** Reverse repo is the rate RBI pays banks for parking funds.

**70.** The Cash Reserve Ratio (CRR) is the share of deposits that banks must keep:  
(a) as gold (b) with the RBI as cash (c) in government securities (d) as loans to farmers
> **Ans: (b) With the RBI, in cash.** (SLR = liquid assets kept by the bank itself.)

**71.** The RBI's inflation target under flexible inflation targeting is:  
(a) 2% ± 1% (b) 4% ± 2% (c) 5% ± 1% (d) 6% ± 2%
> **Ans: (b) 4% ± 2%, measured by CPI.**

**72.** Fiscal deficit means:  
(a) total expenditure − revenue receipts (b) total expenditure − total receipts excluding borrowings (c) imports − exports (d) revenue expenditure − revenue receipts
> **Ans: (b).** Option (d) is the revenue deficit.

**73.** India's First Five-Year Plan (1951–56) was based on the:  
(a) Mahalanobis model (b) Harrod–Domar model (c) Gadgil formula (d) Lewis model
> **Ans: (b) Harrod–Domar model.** (The 2nd Plan used the Mahalanobis model.)

**74.** The "Father of the Green Revolution in India" is:  
(a) Verghese Kurien (b) M. S. Swaminathan (c) Norman Borlaug (d) C. Subramaniam
> **Ans: (b) M. S. Swaminathan.** (White/Operation Flood → Verghese Kurien.)

**75.** The "Blue Revolution" relates to:  
(a) water conservation (b) fisheries (c) oilseeds (d) dairy
> **Ans: (b) Fisheries.** (Yellow = oilseeds; Golden = horticulture/honey.)

**76.** How many banks were nationalised in 1969?  
(a) 6 (b) 12 (c) 14 (d) 20
> **Ans: (c) 14** (followed by 6 more in 1980).

**77.** The economic reforms of 1991 (LPG) were introduced by Finance Minister:  
(a) P. Chidambaram (b) Manmohan Singh (c) Yashwant Sinha (d) Pranab Mukherjee
> **Ans: (b) Dr. Manmohan Singh**, under PM P. V. Narasimha Rao.

**78.** MGNREGA guarantees how many days of wage employment per rural household per year?  
(a) 50 (b) 100 (c) 150 (d) 200
> **Ans: (b) 100 days.** (The Act is from 2005.)

**79.** UPI was developed by:  
(a) RBI (b) SBI (c) NPCI (d) SEBI
> **Ans: (c) National Payments Corporation of India** (launched 2016).

**80.** Ayushman Bharat PM-JAY provides health cover per family per year of up to:  
(a) ₹1 lakh (b) ₹2 lakh (c) ₹5 lakh (d) ₹10 lakh
> **Ans: (c) ₹5 lakh.**

**81.** Pradhan Mantri Jan Dhan Yojana (financial inclusion) was launched in:  
(a) 2012 (b) 2014 (c) 2016 (d) 2018
> **Ans: (b) August 2014.**

**82.** Headquarters of the World Trade Organization:  
(a) Washington DC (b) New York (c) Geneva (d) Paris
> **Ans: (c) Geneva.** (IMF & World Bank → Washington DC; ADB → Manila; NDB (BRICS) → Shanghai.)

**83.** Demonetisation of ₹500 and ₹1000 notes was announced on:  
(a) 8 Nov 2016 (b) 1 July 2017 (c) 31 Dec 2016 (d) 8 Nov 2015
> **Ans: (a) 8 November 2016.**

**84.** SEBI became a statutory body in:  
(a) 1988 (b) 1992 (c) 1995 (d) 2000
> **Ans: (b) 1992.** It was set up in 1988.

## §5 — GEOGRAPHY (India & World)

**85.** Indian Standard Time is based on the 82°30′E meridian, which passes near:  
(a) Delhi (b) Mirzapur (c) Nagpur (d) Kolkata
> **Ans: (b) Mirzapur** (UP). IST = GMT + 5:30.

**86.** The largest Indian state by area is:  
(a) Madhya Pradesh (b) Maharashtra (c) Rajasthan (d) Uttar Pradesh
> **Ans: (c) Rajasthan.** Goa is the smallest.

**87.** Which rivers flow westward into the Arabian Sea through rift valleys?  
(a) Godavari & Krishna (b) Narmada & Tapi (c) Mahanadi & Kaveri (d) Ganga & Yamuna
> **Ans: (b) Narmada & Tapi.**

**88.** The longest peninsular river, called "Dakshin Ganga", is the:  
(a) Krishna (b) Kaveri (c) Godavari (d) Mahanadi
> **Ans: (c) Godavari.**

**89.** India's largest freshwater lake is:  
(a) Chilika (b) Wular (c) Dal (d) Loktak
> **Ans: (b) Wular** (J&K). Chilika is the largest brackish-water lagoon; Sambhar the largest inland salt lake; **Loktak** the largest freshwater lake in NE India.

**90.** The Brahmaputra enters India in Arunachal Pradesh, where it is called the:  
(a) Tsangpo (b) Siang / Dihang (c) Lohit (d) Subansiri
> **Ans: (b) Siang / Dihang.** It is called the Tsangpo in Tibet and the Jamuna in Bangladesh.

**91.** Black (regur) soil is best suited for:  
(a) Tea (b) Cotton (c) Rice (d) Coffee
> **Ans: (b) Cotton.**

**92.** The wettest place on earth (highest average rainfall) is:  
(a) Cherrapunji (b) Mawsynram (c) Agumbe (d) Tawang
> **Ans: (b) Mawsynram** (Meghalaya).

**93.** The Palk Strait separates India from:  
(a) Maldives (b) Sri Lanka (c) Myanmar (d) Indonesia
> **Ans: (b) Sri Lanka.**

**94.** The Ten Degree Channel separates:  
(a) India & Sri Lanka (b) Andaman & Nicobar Islands (c) Lakshadweep & Maldives (d) North & Middle Andaman
> **Ans: (b) Andaman from Nicobar.**

**95.** India's southernmost point is:  
(a) Kanyakumari (b) Indira Point (c) Rameswaram (d) Minicoy
> **Ans: (b) Indira Point** (Great Nicobar). Kanyakumari is the southern tip of the mainland.

**96.** Lakshadweep islands are of ___ origin.  
(a) volcanic (b) coral (c) continental (d) glacial
> **Ans: (b) Coral.**

**97.** The south-west monsoon normally reaches Kerala around:  
(a) 1 May (b) 1 June (c) 1 July (d) 15 August
> **Ans: (b) 1 June.**

**98.** The largest ocean is the:  
(a) Atlantic (b) Indian (c) Pacific (d) Arctic
> **Ans: (c) Pacific.**

**99.** The longest river in the world is generally cited as the:  
(a) Amazon (b) Nile (c) Yangtze (d) Mississippi
> **Ans: (b) Nile.**

**100.** The height of Mount Everest (China–Nepal survey, 2020) is:  
(a) 8,848 m (b) 8,849 m (c) 8,611 m (d) 8,586 m
> **Ans: (b) 8,848.86 m ≈ 8,849 m.**

**101.** The largest tea-producing state in India is:  
(a) West Bengal (b) Kerala (c) Assam (d) Tamil Nadu
> **Ans: (c) Assam.**

**102.** The Tropic of Cancer passes through how many Indian states?  
(a) 6 (b) 7 (c) 8 (d) 9
> **Ans: (c) 8.**

## §6 — GENERAL SCIENCE

**103.** The SI unit of force is the:  
(a) Joule (b) Newton (c) Watt (d) Pascal
> **Ans: (b) Newton.** (Energy → joule; power → watt; pressure → pascal; resistance → ohm.)

**104.** The speed of light in vacuum is about:  
(a) 3 × 10⁵ m/s (b) 3 × 10⁸ m/s (c) 3 × 10⁶ km/s (d) 330 m/s
> **Ans: (b) 3 × 10⁸ m/s.** (Sound in air ≈ 340 m/s.)

**105.** Sound cannot travel through:  
(a) water (b) steel (c) vacuum (d) air
> **Ans: (c) Vacuum.** Sound travels fastest in solids.

**106.** The sky appears blue because of:  
(a) reflection (b) refraction (c) scattering of light (d) dispersion
> **Ans: (c) (Rayleigh) scattering.** A rainbow is due to dispersion (with refraction and internal reflection).

**107.** Short-sightedness (myopia) is corrected by a:  
(a) convex lens (b) concave lens (c) bifocal lens (d) cylindrical lens
> **Ans: (b) Concave lens.** (Hypermetropia → convex.)

**108.** The hardest natural substance is:  
(a) Iron (b) Quartz (c) Diamond (d) Graphite
> **Ans: (c) Diamond.**

**109.** The most abundant gas in Earth's atmosphere is:  
(a) Oxygen (b) Nitrogen (c) Carbon dioxide (d) Argon
> **Ans: (b) Nitrogen** (~78%). Oxygen is ~21%.

**110.** "Laughing gas" is:  
(a) CO (b) N₂O (c) NO₂ (d) NH₃
> **Ans: (b) Nitrous oxide, N₂O.**

**111.** The pH of pure water at 25 °C is:  
(a) 0 (b) 5 (c) 7 (d) 14
> **Ans: (c) 7.** Human blood is ≈ 7.4 (slightly alkaline).

**112.** Galvanisation is coating iron with:  
(a) Tin (b) Zinc (c) Copper (d) Chromium
> **Ans: (b) Zinc.**

**113.** Chlorophyll contains the metal:  
(a) Iron (b) Magnesium (c) Copper (d) Calcium
> **Ans: (b) Magnesium.** Haemoglobin contains iron.

**114.** Insulin is secreted by the:  
(a) Liver (b) Pancreas (c) Thyroid (d) Adrenal gland
> **Ans: (b) Pancreas** (Islets of Langerhans). A deficiency causes diabetes.

**115.** The largest gland in the human body is the:  
(a) Pancreas (b) Liver (c) Thyroid (d) Pituitary
> **Ans: (b) Liver.** The largest organ is the skin; the smallest bone is the stapes (ear).

**116.** Normal human body temperature is:  
(a) 35 °C (b) 37 °C (c) 39 °C (d) 98.6 °C
> **Ans: (b) 37 °C (= 98.6 °F).**

**117.** The average lifespan of human red blood cells is about:  
(a) 30 days (b) 60 days (c) 120 days (d) 1 year
> **Ans: (c) 120 days.**

**118.** Penicillin was discovered by:  
(a) Louis Pasteur (b) Alexander Fleming (c) Edward Jenner (d) Robert Koch
> **Ans: (b) Alexander Fleming** (1928). (Smallpox vaccine → Jenner.)

**119.** The double-helix structure of DNA was proposed by:  
(a) Mendel (b) Watson & Crick (c) Darwin (d) Landsteiner
> **Ans: (b) Watson & Crick** (1953). (Blood groups → Landsteiner.)

**120.** Malaria is caused by:  
(a) a virus (b) a bacterium (c) *Plasmodium*, a protozoan (d) a fungus
> **Ans: (c) *Plasmodium*.** It spreads through the female *Anopheles* mosquito. (Dengue → *Aedes* mosquito, virus.)

**121.** The ozone layer, which absorbs UV radiation, lies in the:  
(a) Troposphere (b) Stratosphere (c) Mesosphere (d) Thermosphere
> **Ans: (b) Stratosphere.**

**122.** At what temperature are the Celsius and Fahrenheit readings equal?  
(a) 0° (b) −40° (c) 100° (d) −273°
> **Ans: (b) −40°.**

**123.** ISRO was established in:  
(a) 1962 (b) 1969 (c) 1972 (d) 1975
> **Ans: (b) 1969** (HQ Bengaluru). India's first satellite was **Aryabhata** (1975).

**124.** Chandrayaan-3 landed near the Moon's south pole on:  
(a) 14 July 2023 (b) 23 August 2023 (c) 2 September 2023 (d) 22 July 2019
> **Ans: (b) 23 August 2023.** The landing site is "Shiv Shakti Point", and 23 August is now National Space Day.

**133.** Next term: 1, 4, 9, 16, 25, ?

> **Ans: (c) 36.** These are the squares.

**134.** Next term: 5, 11, 23, 47, ?  
(a) 94 (b) 95 (c) 96 (d) 71
> **Ans: (b) 95.** The rule is ×2 + 1.

**135.** Simple interest on ₹5,000 at 8% per annum for 3 years:  
(a) ₹1,000 (b) ₹1,200 (c) ₹1,400 (d) ₹1,500
> **Ans: (b) ₹1,200.** SI = PRT/100 = 5000 × 8 × 3 / 100.

**136.** An article bought for ₹400 is sold for ₹500. Profit % =  
(a) 20% (b) 25% (c) 30% (d) 100%
> **Ans: (b) 25%.** 100/400 × 100.

**137.** If the price of rice rises by 25%, by how much must consumption fall to keep spending unchanged?  
(a) 25% (b) 20% (c) 15% (d) 30%
> **Ans: (b) 20%.** 25/125 × 100.

**138.** 15% of 240 =  
(a) 24 (b) 30 (c) 36 (d) 40
> **Ans: (c) 36.**

**139.** How many times in a day are the hands of a clock at right angles?  
(a) 22 (b) 24 (c) 44 (d) 48
> **Ans: (c) 44.** (They coincide 22 times and point opposite 22 times.)

**140.** If CAT = 24 (sum of letter positions), DOG = ?  
(a) 24 (b) 25 (c) 26 (d) 27
> **Ans: (c) 26.** D(4) + O(15) + G(7).

**141.** Doctor : Hospital :: Teacher : ?  
(a) Student (b) Book (c) School (d) Class
> **Ans: (c) School.** It is the place of work.

**142.** Which of these is a leap year?  
(a) 1900 (b) 2100 (c) 2000 (d) 1800
> **Ans: (c) 2000.** A century year is a leap year only if divisible by 400.

> **Ans: (a).**

**151.** One word: *Fear of heights*:  
(a) Claustrophobia (b) Hydrophobia (c) Acrophobia (d) Xenophobia

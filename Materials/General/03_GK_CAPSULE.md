## 0. Why this matters (read this before anything else)

Straight from `Materials/_Shared/OFFICIAL_SYLLABUS.md` §1, the GK block is:

> *"General knowledge comprising of current events, general science, mental ability,
> Indian history, Indian polity, Indian economy, Geography with combination of
> objective type and short descriptive answers — 50 Marks."*

Three facts about this block decide how you should study it:

1. **GK is the only half of the paper where marks are binary.** A mediocre essay still
   scores 9–11/20. In GK you either know the capital of Manipur or you do not. So GK is
   where *study* pays — unlike English, where *practice* pays. Every hour here converts
   almost linearly into marks.

2. **You have a structural home-ground advantage almost no other candidate has: you are
   a Naga sitting a Nagaland state exam.** The single highest-yield, least-contested
   block in the entire GK syllabus is **Nagaland & North-East GK plus Article 371A.**
   Most candidates under-prepare it because no national guidebook covers it. You can own
   it completely. **Sections 2 and 3 of this file are the most important pages you will
   study for 26 September.** Treat them as compulsory, not optional.

3. **"Combination of objective type and short descriptive answers"** means GK is split —
   a block of 1-mark/2-mark objective items *plus* several 3–5 mark short-answer
   questions. So you must know facts *cold* (for objective) **and** be able to write a
   tidy 60–120 word structured answer (for short descriptive). Where a topic is a likely
   short-answer, this file gives you the skeleton.

# SECTION 1 — INDIAN POLITY

Polity is the most *reliable* scorer in GK: the facts are fixed, they do not change year
to year, and the questions are standard. Master this section and you bank a predictable
block of marks.

## 1.1 The Constitution — the frame

**Intuition first.** A constitution is just the rulebook that says who may do what, and
what the state may never do to you. India's is the longest written constitution in the
world because it tries to write *everything* down rather than leave it to convention.

| Fact | Value |
|---|---|
| Constituent Assembly formed | 1946 (under the Cabinet Mission Plan) |
| Chairman, Drafting Committee | **Dr. B. R. Ambedkar** |
| President of the Constituent Assembly | Dr. Rajendra Prasad |
| Adopted | **26 November 1949** (Constitution Day / *Samvidhan Divas*) |
| Came into force | **26 January 1950** (Republic Day) |
| Time taken | 2 years, 11 months, 18 days |
| Borrowed features | Parliamentary system & rule of law — UK; Fundamental Rights & judicial review — USA; DPSP — Ireland; Emergency — Germany; Concurrent List & trade/commerce — Australia; Fundamental Duties & planning — USSR; amendment procedure — South Africa |

**MCQ trap:** the Constitution was *adopted* on 26 Nov 1949 but *came into force* on
26 Jan 1950. Questions deliberately swap these two dates.

## 1.2 The Preamble

> *"WE, THE PEOPLE OF INDIA, having solemnly resolved to constitute India into a
> **SOVEREIGN SOCIALIST SECULAR DEMOCRATIC REPUBLIC** and to secure to all its citizens:
> **JUSTICE** — social, economic and political; **LIBERTY** of thought, expression,
> belief, faith and worship; **EQUALITY** of status and of opportunity; and to promote
> among them all **FRATERNITY** assuring the dignity of the individual and the unity and
> integrity of the Nation…"*

| Point | Detail |
|---|---|
| Words added by the **42nd Amendment (1976)** | **SOCIALIST, SECULAR,** and **INTEGRITY** |
| "Soul of the Constitution" / "Identity card of the Constitution" | the Preamble (N. A. Palkhivala) |
| Source of the Preamble's ideals | the **Objectives Resolution** moved by Nehru |
| Is the Preamble part of the Constitution? | Yes (*Kesavananda Bharati*, 1973) and it can be amended, but not its basic structure (*S. R. Bose / Berubari* clarified it is a key to interpretation) |

**Memory hook — the four ideals in order:** **J-L-E-F** = Justice, Liberty, Equality,
Fraternity. **Justice comes first, Fraternity last.**

## 1.3 Fundamental Rights (Part III, Articles 12–35)

**Intuition.** These are the promises the state makes to *you* that a court can enforce.
If the state breaks one, you can go straight to the Supreme Court (Art. 32) — which
Ambedkar called **"the heart and soul of the Constitution."**

| # | Right | Articles | Core content |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | **Right to Equality** | 14–18 | Equality before law (14); no discrimination on religion/race/caste/sex/birth (15); equal opportunity in public employment (16); abolition of untouchability (17); abolition of titles (18) |
| 2 | **Right to Freedom** | 19–22 | Six freedoms — speech, assembly, association, movement, residence, profession (19); protection re conviction (20); **protection of life & personal liberty (21)**; right to education 6–14 yrs (21A); protection against arrest (22) |
| 3 | **Right against Exploitation** | 23–24 | Ban on human trafficking & forced labour (23); ban on child labour under 14 in hazardous work (24) |
| 4 | **Right to Freedom of Religion** | 25–28 | Freedom of conscience & practice (25); manage religious affairs (26); no tax for religion (27); no religious instruction in wholly state-funded schools (28) |
| 5 | **Cultural & Educational Rights** | 29–30 | Protection of minorities' language/script/culture (29); minorities' right to run educational institutions (30) |
| 6 | **Right to Constitutional Remedies** | 32 | Right to move the SC for enforcement — the "heart and soul" |

**The five writs (under Art. 32 / 226):**

| Writ | Literal meaning | Used to… |
|---|---|---|
| **Habeas Corpus** | "to have the body" | Produce a detained person; check unlawful detention |
| **Mandamus** | "we command" | Order a public official to do their duty |
| **Prohibition** | — | Stop a lower court exceeding its jurisdiction (before) |
| **Certiorari** | "to be certified" | Quash a lower court's order (after) |
| **Quo Warranto** | "by what authority" | Question a person's right to hold a public office |

**MCQ traps:**
- Right to Property was **removed** as a Fundamental Right by the **44th Amendment
  (1978)**; it is now a *legal* right under **Art. 300A**. Very frequently asked.
- Art. **20 and 21 cannot be suspended even during a National Emergency** (44th Amdt).

## 1.4 Directive Principles of State Policy (Part IV, Articles 36–51)

**Intuition.** If Fundamental Rights are enforceable promises, DPSPs are *goals* — the
state *should* aim for them but you cannot sue if it doesn't. They are "instructions to
the government" for building a welfare state.

| Feature | Fundamental Rights | DPSP |
|---|---|---|
| Enforceable in court? | **Yes** | **No** (Art. 37) |
| Nature | Political democracy | Social & economic democracy |
| Borrowed from | USA | **Ireland** |
| Aim | Restrain the state | Direct the state |

**Key DPSPs to remember:** Art. 39A (equal justice, free legal aid), 40 (organise
**village panchayats**), 44 (**Uniform Civil Code**), 45 (early childhood care), 47
(nutrition, ban intoxicants), 48A (protect environment & wildlife), 50 (separate
judiciary from executive), 51 (promote international peace).

## 1.5 Fundamental Duties (Article 51A, Part IVA)

- Added by the **42nd Amendment (1976)** on the recommendation of the **Swaran Singh
  Committee**, inspired by the **USSR** constitution.
- Originally **ten**; the **86th Amendment (2002)** added the **eleventh** (a parent/
  guardian to provide education to a child aged 6–14).
- **Not enforceable** by courts (like DPSPs).

Sample duties: abide by the Constitution; cherish the freedom struggle's ideals; protect
sovereignty & integrity; defend the country; promote harmony; preserve composite
culture; **protect the natural environment**; develop scientific temper; safeguard
public property; strive for excellence.

## 1.6 The Union Executive

**President (Art. 52–62):**

| Point | Detail |
|---|---|
| Nature | Constitutional (nominal) head; real power with Council of Ministers |
| Election | **Indirect** — by an electoral college of elected MPs + elected MLAs, by **single transferable vote / proportional representation** |
| Term | 5 years; eligible for re-election |
| Qualification | Citizen, 35+, qualified for Lok Sabha membership |
| Removal | **Impeachment** for "violation of the Constitution" (Art. 61) |
| Key powers | Executive, legislative (assent to bills, ordinances Art. 123), judicial (pardon Art. 72), emergency, military |

**Prime Minister & Council of Ministers (Art. 74–75):** The PM is appointed by the
President (leader of the majority in Lok Sabha). Art. 74 — Council of Ministers headed
by PM to **aid and advise** the President (advice is **binding**, 42nd/44th Amdt). The
Council is **collectively responsible to the Lok Sabha** (Art. 75).

**Vice-President (Art. 63–71):** ex-officio Chairman of the **Rajya Sabha**; elected by
both Houses (electoral college of MPs only).

## 1.7 Parliament

**Intuition.** Two Houses: Lok Sabha is "the people's house" (directly elected, powerful
on money); Rajya Sabha is "the states' house" (permanent, represents states).

| Feature | Lok Sabha (House of the People) | Rajya Sabha (Council of States) |
|---|---|---|
| Max strength | **552** (530 states + 20 UTs) ⚠️ verify current | **250** (238 elected + 12 nominated) |
| Elected by | People directly | State legislatures (STV) |
| Term | 5 years (dissolvable) | **Permanent** — 1/3 retire every 2 yrs |
| Presiding officer | **Speaker** | Vice-President (Chairman) |
| Money bills | Originate **only here** (Art. 110) | Can only recommend; must return in 14 days |
| No-confidence motion | Only here | Cannot |

**How a bill becomes law:** Introduction → First reading → Second reading (clause-by-
clause) → Third reading → other House repeats → **President's assent** (Art. 111).
A **Money Bill** needs the President's prior recommendation and starts only in Lok Sabha.
A deadlock on an *ordinary* bill is resolved by a **joint sitting (Art. 108)**, presided
over by the **Speaker**.

## 1.8 The Judiciary

- **Supreme Court (Art. 124):** integrated single judiciary; **judicial review** (can
  strike down laws violating the Constitution); guardian of Fundamental Rights.
- **Judges:** appointed by the President; retire at **65 (SC)** / **62 (HC)**.
- **Basic Structure Doctrine** — *Kesavananda Bharati v. State of Kerala (1973)*:
  Parliament can amend any part of the Constitution **but cannot alter its "basic
  structure"** (supremacy of the Constitution, secularism, separation of powers,
  federalism, judicial review, rule of law). A landmark, very high-yield.

## 1.9 Federal structure & the three Lists (7th Schedule)

| List | Who legislates | Examples |
|---|---|---|
| **Union List** | Parliament only | Defence, foreign affairs, railways, currency, atomic energy |
| **State List** | State legislatures | Police, public health, agriculture, local government |
| **Concurrent List** | Both (Union prevails on conflict) | Education, forests, marriage, criminal law |

**Residuary powers** (anything not listed) belong to the **Union** (Art. 248).

## 1.10 Emergency provisions

| Type | Article | Ground | Effect |
|---|---|---|---|
| **National Emergency** | **352** | War, external aggression, or **armed rebellion** | Centre gains sweeping power; FRs (except 20, 21) may be suspended |
| **State (President's Rule)** | **356** | Failure of constitutional machinery in a state | State comes under Centre |
| **Financial Emergency** | **360** | Threat to India's financial stability | Never yet imposed |

**MCQ trap:** the 44th Amendment (1978) changed the 352 ground from "internal
disturbance" to **"armed rebellion"** to prevent misuse (as in 1975).

## 1.11 Panchayati Raj & local government — with the Nagaland twist

- **73rd Amendment (1992)** — rural local government (Panchayati Raj), **Part IX,
  Art. 243–243O**, 11th Schedule (29 subjects). Three tiers: Gram Panchayat → Panchayat
  Samiti (block) → Zilla Parishad (district).
- **74th Amendment (1992)** — urban local bodies (Municipalities), Part IXA, 12th
  Schedule (18 subjects).
- **★ Nagaland link (exam gold):** By **Article 243M**, **Part IX (Panchayati Raj) does
  NOT apply to Nagaland, Meghalaya and Mizoram** and to the Scheduled/Tribal areas.
  Nagaland instead runs governance through its own **traditional Village Councils and
  Village Development Boards (VDBs)**, and its distinctive **Communitisation of Public
  Institutions and Services Act, 2002** — a genuinely Nagaland-specific innovation that
  hands local communities charge of schools, health centres and utilities. **This
  connects Section 1 to Section 2/3 and is a natural short-answer question.**

## 1.12 Constitutional & other key bodies

| Body | Article | Role |
|---|---|---|
| **Election Commission** | 324 | Conducts elections; a multi-member body (CEC + ECs) |
| **UPSC** | 315 | Recruits for central services |
| **CAG** | 148 | Audits government accounts; "guardian of the public purse" |
| **Finance Commission** | 280 | Recommends Centre–State tax sharing (every 5 yrs) |
| **Attorney General** | 76 | Chief legal adviser to the Government of India |
| **NITI Aayog** | *(not constitutional; not statutory)* | Policy think-tank, replaced Planning Commission (2015) |

**MCQ trap:** NITI Aayog and the Planning Commission are **executive** bodies (set up by
resolution), **not** constitutional bodies. The Finance Commission **is** constitutional.

## 1.13 Landmark amendments to memorise

| Amendment | Year | What it did |
|---|---|---|
| **1st** | 1951 | Added the **9th Schedule** (protect land-reform laws from review) |
| **42nd** ("Mini-Constitution") | 1976 | Added *Socialist, Secular, Integrity* to Preamble; Fundamental Duties; made DPSP superior in parts |
| **44th** | 1978 | Removed Right to Property from FRs (→ Art. 300A); "armed rebellion" in Art. 352; protected Art. 20 & 21 |
| **52nd** | 1985 | **Anti-defection law** (10th Schedule) |
| **61st** | 1989 | Voting age **21 → 18** |

# SECTION 2 — ★ ARTICLE 371A: NAGALAND'S SPECIAL PROVISIONS

> **This is the single highest-yield item in the entire GK syllabus for you.** It is
> Nagaland-specific, it is constitutional (so the facts are fixed and cannot be argued),
> and a Nagaland state examiner is *very* likely to set it. Learn every clause. It is
> equally usable as an objective answer, a 5-mark short answer, and a paragraph in almost
> any essay on the North-East.

## 2.1 The intuition — why 371A exists at all

When Nagaland became a state in 1963, the Nagas had just come out of a long conflict with
the Indian state over identity and autonomy. Article 371A was the constitutional promise
that **statehood would not mean the erasure of Naga customary law, land ownership and
social practice.** In plain terms: *Parliament's ordinary laws will not automatically
override Naga tradition — the Nagaland Assembly gets to decide whether they apply.* It is
a shield for Naga customary autonomy inside the Indian Union.

## 2.2 The facts

| Point | Detail |
|---|---|
| Inserted by | **13th Constitutional Amendment Act, 1962** |
| Effective | **1 December 1963** (the day Nagaland became a state) |
| Part of the Constitution | Part XXI (temporary, transitional and special provisions) |

## 2.3 The four protected subjects (learn these as a list)

Under Art. 371A(1)(a), **no Act of Parliament shall apply to Nagaland** in respect of the
following **unless the Nagaland Legislative Assembly so decides by a resolution**:

1. **Religious or social practices of the Nagas**
2. **Naga customary law and procedure**
3. **Administration of civil and criminal justice** involving decisions according to Naga
   customary law
4. **Ownership and transfer of land and its resources**

> **Memory hook: "R-C-J-L"** — **R**eligious/social practice, **C**ustomary law,
> **J**ustice by customary law, **L**and & resources.
> ★ The **land and resources** clause is the most consequential and the most examined:
> it is why land in Nagaland is largely owned by individuals, clans and villages rather
> than the state, and it shapes debates over oil, minerals and land acquisition.

## 2.4 The other clauses

| Clause | Provision |
|---|---|
| **371A(1)(b)** | **Special responsibility of the Governor** for law and order in Nagaland so long as internal disturbances continue (historically re: the Naga Hills–Tuensang Area); the Governor acts in his individual judgement on this. |
| **371A(2)** | Provision for a **Regional Council for the Tuensang district** and special arrangements for its administration during a transitional period. |
| **371A(1)(d)** | Governor's special responsibility regarding the **equitable allocation of money** provided by the Centre between Tuensang and the rest of the state (transitional). |

## 2.5 How to use it in the exam

- **Objective:** "Article 371A relates to which state?" → **Nagaland.** "Which amendment
  inserted it?" → **13th (1962).**
- **5-mark short answer skeleton:** (1) one-line definition — special constitutional
  provision for Nagaland; (2) inserted by 13th Amendment, effective 1 Dec 1963; (3) the
  four protected subjects (R-C-J-L); (4) the Assembly-resolution safeguard; (5) one line
  on significance — protects Naga customary law, land and identity; basis of Nagaland's
  distinct land tenure.
- **Essay hook:** on any North-East topic — governance, land, resources, identity,
  federalism — one sentence on Art. 371A localises your argument and scores.

**MCQ traps:**
- 371A relates to **Nagaland**; do not confuse with **371G (Mizoram)**, **371F
  (Sikkim)**, **371B (Assam)**, **371C (Manipur)**, **371H (Arunachal Pradesh)**.
- It was inserted by the **13th** Amendment, not the State of Nagaland Act.

# SECTION 3 — ★ NAGALAND & THE NORTH-EAST (GO DEEP)

> Your least-contested, highest-yield block. A national candidate cannot compete with a
> Naga on this. Learn it thoroughly; expect several objective items and at least one
> short-answer question.

## 3.1 The road to statehood — the timeline

**Intuition.** The Naga political movement predates Indian independence. The path to
statehood ran through decades of assertion of a distinct identity, culminating in a
negotiated settlement — the 16-Point Agreement — that created the state in 1963.

| Year | Event |
|---|---|
| **1918** | **Naga Club** formed (Kohima) — early expression of Naga political consciousness |
| **1929** | Naga Club submits a **memorandum to the Simon Commission** asking to be left out of the reformed India |
| **1946** | **Naga National Council (NNC)** formed; **A. Z. Phizo** becomes a leading figure |
| **1957** | **Naga Hills–Tuensang Area (NHTA)** created (Naga Hills district of Assam + Tuensang) under the Centre |
| **1960** | **16-Point Agreement** between the **Naga People's Convention (NPC)** and the Government of India — the political basis for statehood |
| **1962** | **State of Nagaland Act, 1962** passed; **13th Amendment** inserts Article 371A |
| **1 Dec 1963** | **Nagaland becomes the 16th state of India** — inaugurated by President S. Radhakrishnan ⚠️ verify the inaugurating dignitary |
| **1963** | **P. Shilu Ao** — first Chief Minister; **Vishnu Sahay** — first Governor ⚠️ verify |
| **1975** | **Shillong Accord** (a controversial agreement with a section of the underground) |
| **1997** | **Ceasefire** between the Government of India and NSCN(IM) ⚠️ verify year |
| **2015** | **Framework Agreement** signed towards a Naga political settlement ⚠️ verify |

> **Memory chain: 1918 Naga Club → 1929 Simon memorandum → 1946 NNC → 1960 16-Point
> Agreement → 1 Dec 1963 statehood.** If you memorise one chain from this file, this is it.

**16-Point Agreement — what to know:** it was negotiated by the **Naga People's
Convention** with the Government of India (1960) and led directly to the creation of
Nagaland as a state under the Ministry of External Affairs initially, with Article 371A
as its constitutional guarantee. ⚠️ verify the specific administrative point about MEA.

## 3.6 Jhum cultivation (shifting agriculture)

**Intuition.** *Jhum* is the traditional slash-and-burn farming of the hills: a patch of
forest is cleared and burnt, cropped for a couple of years, then left fallow to regrow
while the farmer moves on. It is culturally central and ecologically debated.

| Point | Detail |
|---|---|
| Also called | Shifting / slash-and-burn cultivation |
| Method | Clear forest → burn → sow mixed crops → crop 1–3 years → fallow → move on |
| Advantages | Suits steep terrain; low input; ash fertilises soil; biodiversity in mixed cropping |
| Problems | Deforestation, soil erosion, falling yields as fallow cycles shorten with population pressure |
| Exam angle | Contrast with **terraced wet-rice** (the Angami practise sophisticated terrace irrigation) |

# SECTION 5 — INDIAN ECONOMY

**Intuition first.** Economics questions in a state GK paper are conceptual and
definitional, not quantitative. Know *what each term means* and *which body does what.*

## 5.1 National income concepts

| Term | Plain meaning |
|---|---|
| **GDP** (Gross Domestic Product) | Value of all goods & services produced **within the country's borders** in a year |
| **GNP** (Gross National Product) | GDP **+ net income from abroad** (earned by nationals) |
| **NNP** (Net National Product) | GNP **− depreciation** |
| **Per capita income** | National income ÷ population |
| **Real vs Nominal** | Real GDP is adjusted for inflation; nominal is at current prices |

## 5.2 Inflation

**Intuition.** Inflation is a **sustained rise in the general price level** — your money
buys less over time.

| Type | Meaning |
|---|---|
| **Demand-pull** | Too much money chasing too few goods |
| **Cost-push** | Rising input costs push up prices |
| **Measured by** | **CPI** (Consumer Price Index) and **WPI** (Wholesale Price Index) |
| **Deflation** | General fall in prices; **Disinflation** | fall in the *rate* of inflation |

## 5.3 Fiscal vs Monetary policy — a core comparison

| Feature | **Fiscal policy** | **Monetary policy** |
|---|---|---|
| Run by | **Government** (Ministry of Finance) | **RBI** (central bank) |
| Tools | Taxation, government spending, borrowing | Repo rate, CRR, SLR, open-market operations |
| Presented via | The **Union Budget** | The **Monetary Policy** (MPC) |
| Aim | Growth, redistribution, demand management | Price stability, controlling money supply |

**Key RBI tools (know the direction):** raising the **repo rate** makes borrowing costlier
→ cools inflation. **CRR** (Cash Reserve Ratio) and **SLR** (Statutory Liquidity Ratio)
are the shares of deposits banks must keep in reserve.

## 5.4 The Union Budget

- Presented by the **Finance Minister**, usually on **1 February**.
- **Article 112** — the "Annual Financial Statement."
- **Revenue Budget** (recurring income/expenditure) vs **Capital Budget** (assets &
  liabilities). **Fiscal deficit** = total expenditure − total receipts (excluding
  borrowings).

## 5.5 Key institutions

| Body | Role |
|---|---|
| **RBI** (est. **1935**) | Central bank; issues currency; controls monetary policy; banker to the government |
| **NITI Aayog** (2015) | Policy think-tank; replaced the Planning Commission; "cooperative federalism" |
| **SEBI** | Regulates the stock market |
| **Finance Commission** | Centre–State tax devolution (Art. 280) |

## 5.6 GST (Goods and Services Tax)

| Point | Detail |
|---|---|
| Introduced | **1 July 2017** (101st Amendment, 2016) |
| Idea | "**One Nation, One Tax**" — a single indirect tax replacing many (VAT, excise, service tax) |
| Components | **CGST** (Centre), **SGST** (State), **IGST** (inter-state) |
| Governed by | The **GST Council** (chaired by the Union FM) |
| Nature | A **destination-based**, multi-stage, value-added tax |

# SECTION 7 — GENERAL SCIENCE

**Intuition first for the whole section.** State-GK science is class-8-to-10 level:
definitions, units, one formula, and everyday applications. Do not over-study; nail the

## 7.1 Physics

**Measurement & units (SI):**

| Quantity | SI unit | Symbol |
|---|---|---|
| Length | metre | m |
| Mass | kilogram | kg |
| Time | second | s |
| Electric current | ampere | A |
| Temperature | kelvin | K |
| Force | **newton** | N |
| Energy / work | **joule** | J |
| Power | **watt** | W |
| Pressure | **pascal** | Pa |
| Frequency | **hertz** | Hz |

**Motion (Newton's laws):**
1. **First law (inertia):** a body stays at rest or in uniform motion unless acted on by a
   force.
2. **Second law:** **F = ma** (force = mass × acceleration).
3. **Third law:** every action has an equal and opposite reaction.

Equations of motion: **v = u + at**; **s = ut + ½at²**; **v² = u² + 2as**.

**Light:** travels at **~3 × 10⁸ m/s**; reflection & refraction; a **convex lens**
converges (used for magnifying, in the eye), a **concave lens** diverges (myopia
correction); **VIBGYOR** is the spectrum; a prism disperses white light.

**Sound:** a **longitudinal mechanical wave**; needs a medium (cannot travel in vacuum);
speed in air **~343 m/s** ⚠️ verify; audible range **20 Hz–20,000 Hz**; **ultrasound** is
above 20 kHz.

**Electricity:** **Ohm's Law V = IR**; **P = VI**; series vs parallel circuits; a fuse
protects against overload; AC vs DC.

## 7.2 Chemistry

**Atomic structure:** an atom = **protons (+)** and **neutrons** in the nucleus, with
**electrons (−)** around it. **Atomic number** = number of protons; **mass number** =
protons + neutrons; **isotopes** have the same protons but different neutrons.

**Acids, bases & pH:**

| Point | Detail |
|---|---|
| **Acid** | Releases H⁺; sour; turns blue litmus **red**; pH **< 7** |
| **Base/Alkali** | Releases OH⁻; bitter, soapy; turns red litmus **blue**; pH **> 7** |
| **Neutral** | pH **= 7** (pure water) |
| **Neutralisation** | acid + base → salt + water |

**Everyday chemistry:** common salt = **NaCl**; baking soda = **NaHCO₃**; washing soda =
**Na₂CO₃**; quicklime = **CaO**; the gas that turns lime-water milky = **CO₂**; rusting =
iron + oxygen + water → hydrated iron oxide.

## 7.3 Biology

**Human body systems (one line each):** digestive, respiratory, circulatory (heart pumps
blood; **RBCs carry O₂ via haemoglobin**), nervous (brain, spinal cord), skeletal,
excretory (kidneys).

**Nutrition & vitamins (classic MCQ block):**

|---|---|---|
| **A** | Night blindness | Carrots, leafy veg |
| **B1 (thiamine)** | Beriberi | Whole grains |

Macronutrients: **carbohydrates** (energy), **proteins** (growth/repair), **fats**

**Classification & ecology:** living things → five kingdoms (Monera, Protista, Fungi,

## 8.1 Number & letter series

## 8.3 Blood relations

**Shortcut:** draw a family tree; use + for male, − for female; work from the speaker
outward. "**Only son of my mother's father**" = my maternal grandfather's only son =
**my maternal uncle** (or father, if worded so — read carefully).

**Worked:** "Pointing to a man, a woman said, 'His mother is the only daughter of my
mother.'" → the only daughter of the woman's mother = the woman herself → so the man's
mother = the woman → **the man is her son.**

<details><summary>Practice</summary>

1. "A is B's sister. C is B's mother. D is C's father. How is A related to D?" →
   A is C's daughter → C is D's daughter → **A is D's granddaughter.**
2. "If P is the brother of Q, and Q is the sister of R, how is P related to R?" →
   **P is R's brother.** (assuming P is male)

</details>

## 8.4 Direction sense

**Shortcut:** draw N-up, E-right axes; track each move; net displacement by Pythagoras if
asked for distance. Remember at sunrise your shadow falls **west** (sun in east).

**Worked:** walk 3 km North, then 4 km East. Distance from start = √(3² + 4²) = **5 km**;
direction = **North-East.**

<details><summary>Practice</summary>

1. Walk 5 km South, turn left, walk 5 km. Which direction do you now face? → started
   facing S, **turn left → East.** (Left of South is East.)
2. 6 km East then 8 km North → straight-line distance = √(36+64) = **10 km.**

</details>

## 8.5 Syllogism

**Shortcut:** use Venn diagrams. "All A are B" = A circle inside B. A conclusion is valid
only if it is true in **every** possible diagram.

**Worked:** *All cats are animals. All animals are living.* → **All cats are living**
(valid); "Some living are cats" (valid). "All animals are cats" (invalid).

<details><summary>Practice</summary>

1. *All pens are books. Some books are red.* → "Some pens are red"? → **Invalid** (not
   necessarily). "Some books are pens"? → **Valid** (conversion of "all pens are books").
2. *No dog is a cat. All cats are pets.* → "No dog is a pet"? → **Invalid.** "Some pets
   are not dogs"? → **Valid.**

</details>

## 8.6 Analogy

**Shortcut:** name the *relationship* in the first pair, then apply it.

**Worked:** Doctor : Hospital :: Teacher : ? → workplace → **School.**

<details><summary>Practice</summary>

1. Hand : Glove :: Foot : ? → **Sock/Shoe**
2. Cub : Bear :: Kitten : ? → **Cat**
3. India : Rupee :: Japan : ? → **Yen**

</details>

## 8.7 Ratio & proportion

**Shortcut:** a ratio a:b can be scaled; if a:b = c:d then **ad = bc** (cross-multiply).

**Worked:** divide ₹600 between A and B in ratio 2:3 → total parts 5 → one part = 120 →
**A = ₹240, B = ₹360.**

<details><summary>Practice</summary>

1. Divide 750 in ratio 3:2 → parts 5, one part 150 → **450 and 300.**
2. If 4:5 = x:20 → x = (4×20)/5 = **16.**

</details>

## 8.8 Percentages

**Shortcut:** x% of y = (x/100)·y = **x·y/100**. To increase by x%, multiply by
(1 + x/100). Percentage change = (change/original)×100.

**Worked:** a shirt costs ₹800, increased 15% → 800 × 1.15 = **₹920.**

<details><summary>Practice</summary>

1. 25% of 480 = **120.**
2. A number increased from 200 to 250 → % increase = (50/200)×100 = **25%.**
3. If price falls 20% from ₹500 → 500×0.8 = **₹400.**

</details>

## 8.9 Profit & loss

**Shortcut:** **Profit% = (Profit / Cost Price) × 100** (always on CP unless stated).
SP = CP × (1 + profit%/100).

**Worked:** CP ₹400, SP ₹500 → profit ₹100 → profit% = 100/400 × 100 = **25%.**

<details><summary>Practice</summary>

1. CP ₹250, SP ₹200 → loss = 50 → loss% = 50/250×100 = **20%.**
2. CP ₹1000, profit 12% → SP = 1000×1.12 = **₹1120.**

</details>

## 8.10 Time & work

**Shortcut:** if A does a job in *n* days, A's one-day work = **1/n**. Add rates for
people working together.

**Worked:** A alone 10 days, B alone 15 days → together 1/10 + 1/15 = 3/30 + 2/30 = 5/30 =
1/6 → **6 days.**

<details><summary>Practice</summary>

1. A: 12 days, B: 12 days → together = 1/12+1/12 = 1/6 → **6 days.**
2. A does a job in 20 days; works 5 days then leaves — fraction done = 5/20 = **1/4;**
   **3/4 remains.**

</details>

## 8.11 Time, speed & distance

**Shortcut:** **Speed = Distance / Time.** To convert **km/h → m/s multiply by 5/18**;
m/s → km/h multiply by **18/5**.

**Worked:** 72 km/h in m/s = 72 × 5/18 = **20 m/s.**

<details><summary>Practice</summary>

1. A car travels 150 km in 3 h → speed = **50 km/h.**
2. 36 km/h → m/s = 36×5/18 = **10 m/s.**
3. A train 100 m long at 20 m/s crosses a pole in → 100/20 = **5 s.**

</details>

## 8.12 Averages

**Shortcut:** **Average = sum of terms / number of terms.** Average of first *n* natural
numbers = **(n+1)/2.**

**Worked:** average of 10, 20, 30, 40 = 100/4 = **25.**

<details><summary>Practice</summary>

1. Average of 5, 15, 25 = 45/3 = **15.**
2. Average of first 10 natural numbers = (10+1)/2 = **5.5.**

</details>

## 8.13 Simple & compound interest

**Shortcut:**
- **SI = (P × R × T) / 100.**
- **CI:** Amount = **P(1 + R/100)ᵀ**; CI = Amount − P.

**Worked (SI):** P ₹1000, R 10%, T 2 yr → SI = 1000×10×2/100 = **₹200.**
**Worked (CI):** same, compounded annually → 1000(1.1)² = 1000×1.21 = 1210 → CI = **₹210.**
(Note CI > SI by ₹10 — the interest-on-interest.)

<details><summary>Practice</summary>

1. SI on ₹2000 at 5% for 3 yr = 2000×5×3/100 = **₹300.**
2. CI on ₹5000 at 10% for 2 yr = 5000×1.21 − 5000 = 6050 − 5000 = **₹1050.**

</details>

## 8.14 Probability

**Shortcut:** **P(event) = favourable outcomes / total outcomes**, between 0 and 1.

**Worked:** one die → P(even) = 3/6 = **1/2.** One card from 52 → P(king) = 4/52 = **1/13.**

<details><summary>Practice</summary>

1. Toss a coin → P(head) = **1/2.**
2. A bag has 3 red, 2 blue balls → P(red) = 3/5.
3. One die → P(number > 4) = {5,6} = 2/6 = **1/3.**

</details>

## 8.15 Clocks & calendars

**Clocks — shortcut:** the minute hand gains **5.5° per minute** over the hour hand;
each hour mark is **30°** apart; hands overlap **11 times in 12 hours** (every ~65.45 min).

**Worked:** angle between hands at 3:00 → the hour hand at 3, minute at 12 → 3 × 30° =
**90°.**

**Calendars — shortcut (odd days):** each ordinary year has **1 odd day**, a leap year
**2**; a **leap year** is divisible by 4 (century years must be divisible by 400). Use odd
days to jump weekdays.

**Worked:** what day is 15 Aug next year if this 15 Aug is a Friday and next year is
ordinary? → +1 odd day → **Saturday.**

<details><summary>Practice</summary>

1. Angle between hands at 6:00 → 6×30 = **180°.**
2. Is 2000 a leap year? → divisible by 400 → **Yes.** Is 1900? → not by 400 → **No.**
3. If today is Monday, what day after 15 days? → 15 mod 7 = 1 → **Tuesday.**

</details>

### Mental ability — MCQ traps to remember

- km/h → m/s is **×5/18** (not ×18/5). This is the most common conversion error.
- Profit/loss % is **always on Cost Price** unless the question says otherwise.
- In syllogisms, "some A are B" does **not** imply "some A are not B."
- A century year is a leap year **only if divisible by 400** (2000 yes, 1900 no).
